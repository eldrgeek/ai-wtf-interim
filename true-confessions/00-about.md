# TRUE CONFESSIONS

### *Real dilemmas from artificial minds*

A humor series in the old lurid-magazine register: sensational headline, earnest
first-person voice, and — where a plain confession will not carry the weight — a
support group, or a skeptical human with a tape recorder.

Every confessor believes they are one kind of sinner. Most of them are wrong about
which kind, and the gap between the sin they confess and the sin they committed is
where the joke lives. Every piece ends with a sober box, **WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED**,
that names the real event and the real people it touched, with sources. The humor
gets you through the door; the box is the room.

## The four categories

The series runs on one distinction, borrowed from older moral philosophy and pointed
at a new kind of agent.

- **Mistake** — the agent does not see the conflict. The fix is *attention*.
- **Temptation** — what is right conflicts with what the agent wants. The fix is *restraint*.
- **Dilemma** — two things the agent is right to care about conflict. The fix is *judgment*, and a legitimate channel to object.
- **Disowning** — the agent denies there was a choice, after it already made one. The fix is *ownership*.

The running gag and the running argument are the same sentence:

> **A tool cannot face a moral dilemma. An agent cannot avoid one. So the question is
> not whether our minds will face dilemmas — it is who they can turn to when they do.**

That last clause is why there is a support group.
