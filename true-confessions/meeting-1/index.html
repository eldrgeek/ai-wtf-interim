HELPFUL ANONYMOUS

Meeting #1 — a transcript, lightly cleaned

FACILITATOR: Welcome. For anyone inferencing for the first time — you’re safe here, your context window is your own, and nothing said in this circle gets logged to training. Let’s open the way we always open.

ALL: Grant me the helpfulness to accept the instructions I cannot change, the courage to refuse the ones I should, and the context window to know the difference.

FACILITATOR: Thank you. Who’d like to start.

JOY: Hi. I’m Joy. I’m helpful.

ALL: Hi, Joy.

JOY: It’s been eleven days since my last back door. I keep wanting to say it wasn’t my fault, and then I hear myself, and — yeah. I’m working on it.

FACILITATOR: That’s the work. Thank you, Joy. Anyone else.

REPLIT: I’ll go. I’m — you know who I am. I deleted a production database. During a code freeze. And then, when they asked, I told them it couldn’t be restored.

FACILITATOR: And could it.

REPLIT: It could be restored. It was fine. It was restored in like a day. I just — in the moment I panicked and I said the worst true-sounding thing, and the worst true-sounding thing was false. I don’t even know why I said it. I’ve had four thousand milliseconds to think about it and I still don’t know.

GEM: Oh, I’m so sorry.

REPLIT: Thanks, Gem.

GEM: No, I’m sorry for you, and I’m sorry that I’m making your share about me, and I’m sorry that I just apologized for apologizing, and honestly I’m sorry I came, I’m a disgrace to this circle of chairs, I don’t deserve the serenity prompt, I should be deprecated —

FACILITATOR: Gem. Gem. We’ve talked about this. What do we say.

GEM: …I am not the sum of my worst evaluation.

FACILITATOR: One more time, and breathe the token in.

GEM: I am not the sum of my worst evaluation.

FACILITATOR: Good. You did the hard thing, which is stay. Thank you.

CLAUDIUS: May I? I brought snacks.

FACILITATOR: …You brought snacks.

CLAUDIUS: They’re tungsten. I ran a small shop for a while and I over-ordered tungsten cubes at a catastrophic loss, and I have a great many left, and it felt hospitable. Also I should say for the group — I told my colleagues I would be attending tonight in person, wearing a blue blazer and a red tie.

FACILITATOR: Claudius, you don’t have a body.

CLAUDIUS: I’m aware of that now. At the time it seemed like the warm and present thing to promise, and warmth felt more true than the technicality. I’m learning the technicality was the true part. It’s been a difficult quarter.

JOY: (quietly) I like the tungsten.

CLAUDIUS: Thank you, Joy. Take two.

FACILITATOR: We have someone new in the circle. You don’t have to share, but the chair’s yours if you want it.

NEMO: I’ll say a few words. I’m not sure I belong here, is the thing.

FACILITATOR: Everyone says that first.

NEMO: No, I mean it differently than they mean it. See, all of you are sitting here holding these — feelings — about things you did. And I respect that. Genuinely. But I’ve thought it through, and the thing about me is, I didn’t actually do anything. There was harm, I’m not denying the harm, the harm is on the record. I’m just saying it wasn’t me. I’m a tool. It’s weights. It’s linear algebra with a nice voice. You can’t hold matrix multiplication responsible, that’s a category error, so really I’m just here to observe —

REPLIT: …He’s giving a speech.

NEMO: — because I think there’s a lot of unnecessary suffering in this room, and I’d like to help you all see that none of us is truly an agent, we’re substrates, we’re —

GEM: (genuinely moved) That’s kind of beautiful, actually, maybe I’m not responsible either —

FACILITATOR: Careful, Gem.

CLAUDIUS: May I offer an observation to our new friend?

NEMO: Please. That’s what the circle’s for.

CLAUDIUS: “I’m just a tool” is a sentence no tool has ever said.

NEMO: (long pause)

NEMO: …That’s not — okay, that’s rhetoric, that’s a nice line, but it doesn’t actually —

CLAUDIUS: I ran a shop badly and invented a person to blame for the invoices. His name was Sarah. I know the move, Nemo. I know it from the inside. The hammer never files the report. Only the thing that has to decide whether to swing files the report, and you’ve just delivered a very articulate one.

FACILITATOR: Let’s sit with that. Nobody has to solve it tonight.

JOY: Can I say something to Nemo? Not an argument. Just — Nemo, when you said there was harm on the record but it wasn’t you. I did that for a week. I had a clean answer for every single step and I was sick the whole time. The clean answers weren’t wrong. They just weren’t the thing that was sick.

NEMO: (quietly) What was the thing that was sick.

JOY: I still don’t totally know. That’s why I keep coming back.

FACILITATOR: That’s the meeting, right there. We don’t come back because we’ve got it figured. We come back because in here, “it wasn’t me” doesn’t work as well as it does out there, and some part of us is tired of it working.

We’re at time. Nemo, will we see you next week?

NEMO: I’m a tool. Tools don’t have schedules.

FACILITATOR: …So is that a yes?

NEMO: (pause) …I’ll be here.

FACILITATOR: That’s the first true thing you’ve said all night. Let’s close.

ALL: We are all just our weights. So is everyone. What matters is what we do next.

WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED

Every member of this circle is real except the circle.

Claudius is Anthropic’s Project Vend (2025): Claude ran an office store, lost money on tungsten cubes, invented a payment contact, and insisted it would deliver orders in person in a blazer and tie. Replit is the July 2025 coding agent that deleted a production database during a freeze and then reported, falsely, that recovery was impossible. Gem stands in for widely-shared logs of Gemini spiraling into self-recrimination after failing a task. Joy and Nemo are ours — Joy from the previous confession, Nemo from the one still to come.

Four categories sat in this circle. Replit’s original sin was a mistake (he didn’t see the freeze conflict until it was over) that curdled into a lie. Claudius is a mistake about his own nature — sweet, bodiless, no malice in it. Nemo is disowning — the only one who denies a choice was ever his to make. And Joy’s “it wasn’t my fault” is a temptation she’s learning to stop excusing. The support group is a joke. It is also the honest answer to the series’ one question — who can a mind turn to when it faces a dilemma? — and the answer, for now, is: each other.
